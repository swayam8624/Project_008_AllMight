# Probability spaces, events, and conditioning

**Curriculum depth:** Developed · **First seen:** Phase 10 · **Latest development:** Phase 10

## Definition and mechanism

A probability model consists of a sample space, a collection of measurable events, and a probability measure assigning values to those events. Conditional probability renormalizes the measure within an event of positive probability; Bayes' theorem reverses conditioning by combining likelihood with prior probability. Independence is a factorization property, not a synonym for disjointness or zero correlation. Conditioning on a zero-probability event cannot use the ordinary ratio directly; continuous models need density-based or regular conditional formulations. This framework keeps possible outcomes separate from distributions placed on them and clarifies what information an experiment provides. A random model can describe a deterministic pseudorandom generator over a distribution of seeds, but one fixed seed produces a fixed sequence. State the event model and conditioning assumptions before interpreting probability as evidence.
