# Common probability distributions and their assumptions

**Curriculum depth:** Developed · **First seen:** Phase 10 · **Latest development:** Phase 10

## Definition and mechanism

Distribution families encode different experiment structures. Bernoulli models one binary trial; binomial counts independent trials with the same success probability; uniform assigns constant density or mass over a specified support; Gaussian models a bell-shaped law through mean and covariance; exponential describes memoryless waiting under a constant event rate; Poisson counts events in a fixed exposure under independent, stationary event assumptions. Their formulas are not interchangeable labels: each relies on support and sampling assumptions. Moments summarize center and spread but may fail to exist for heavy-tailed laws. Multivariate Gaussian models also require a symmetric positive-semidefinite covariance, with positive definiteness needed for an ordinary full-dimensional density. Identify the experiment, parameter meanings, and dependence structure before selecting a distribution. The same family can model measurement noise, a prior, or a sampling proposal, which are different roles.
