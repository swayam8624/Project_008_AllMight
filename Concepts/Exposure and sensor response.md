# Exposure and sensor response

**Curriculum depth:** Developed · **First seen:** University courses · **Latest development:** University courses

## Definition and mechanism

A camera sensor converts arriving photons into electrical measurements. Exposure depends on scene radiance, lens transmission, aperture, shutter duration, and sensor sensitivity; changing these settings trades motion blur, depth of field, noise, and saturation. A pixel integrates light over a finite area and exposure interval before quantization. Sensor wells have finite capacity, so highlights clip, while low photon counts have shot noise and readout adds electronic noise. ISO gain amplifies the signal and its noise rather than collecting additional photons. The recorded raw values are not yet display colors: white balance, color transforms, tone curves, and transfer encoding turn measurements into an image. A camera model must distinguish photon capture, sensor response, and display rendering to explain dynamic range and exposure choices.
