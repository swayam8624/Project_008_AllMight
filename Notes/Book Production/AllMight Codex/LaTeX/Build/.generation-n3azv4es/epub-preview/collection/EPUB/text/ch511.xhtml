QR factorization and least squares

QR writes A = Q R with orthonormal columns in Q and triangular R . Orthogonal coordinates preserve Euclidean lengths, so least squares reduces to a triangular solve and a perpendicular residual. Householder QR is a stable dense method; Gram–Schmidt explains the geometry but can lose orthogonality.

Use it when: fitting overdetermined data with known full column rank.

Developed in Phase 8: Orthogonal transformations change coordinates without changing Euclidean lengths. QR therefore separates the fitted component, solved through triangular R , from the constant perpendicular residual; the Householder construction is the stable dense route, with Gram–Schmidt retained to make the geometry visible.
