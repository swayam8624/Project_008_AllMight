Reflection refraction and shading directions

Curriculum depth: Developed · First seen: Phase6 · Latest development: University courses

Definition and mechanism

Plane reflection reverses a direction’s normal component. Refraction scales its tangent component by the incident/transmitted index ratio and solves for a unit normal component. A negative computed discriminant indicates total internal reflection under the stated model. Half-vectors require normalized outward light/view directions.

Incident ( 0.6 , 0 , − 0.8 ) reflects to ( 0.6 , 0 , 0.8 ) across normal ( 0 , 0 , 1 ) . Antiparallel light/view directions have no unique half-vector.

University course development

Extend the existing direction note by separating geometric surface orientation from optical ray behavior. The normal vector is perpendicular to a local tangent plane; it determines the cosine term in diffuse shading and the directions of ideal reflection and refraction. For unit incident direction ω i and unit normal n , specular reflection mirrors the direction about the normal, while refraction changes direction according to the ratio of refractive indices and may undergo total internal reflection. Direction conventions matter: some texts define incoming vectors toward the surface, others point away from it, so signs must be checked before applying formulas. A shading normal may differ from the true geometric normal, as in normal mapping, but it cannot change silhouette or visibility. Distinguish shading perturbations from geometry displacement, and use consistent normals when evaluating BRDFs, ray offsets, and medium transitions.
