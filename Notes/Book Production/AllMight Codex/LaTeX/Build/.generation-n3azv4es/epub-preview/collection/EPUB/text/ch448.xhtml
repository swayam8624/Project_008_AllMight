Eigenvalues and eigenspaces

An eigenvector is a nonzero direction that a square linear map scales by its eigenvalue. The eigenspace is the null space of A − λ I . Real symmetric matrices have an orthonormal eigenbasis; general matrices may be defective or have sensitive eigenvectors.

Use it when: studying repeated powers, invariant directions, covariance, stability, or matrix functions.

Developed in Phase 8: Eigenanalysis begins with invariant directions, then distinguishes algebraic multiplicity from eigenspace dimension and shows why a defective matrix may not have a full eigenbasis.
