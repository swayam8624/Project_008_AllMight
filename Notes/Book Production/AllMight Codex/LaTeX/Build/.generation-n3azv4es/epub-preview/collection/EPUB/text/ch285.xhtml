Camera projection: shortest useful recall

Frames and transforms

  • A coordinate needs a frame: p = o + B p F . Camera world-to-view is p V = R T ( p W − c ) ; points use homogeneous w = 1 , directions use w = 0 , and normals use A − T .
  • Frames and coordinate meaning · Camera pose and view transform

Projection, depth, raster, and culling

  • For RH view space looking down − Z , axial depth is d = − z v and perspective clip w = d . Projection and depth mapping are separate choices; declare NDC range, depth direction, viewport origin, and Y orientation.
  • Recover positions using the rendered projection and matching pixel centre, viewport, and depth convention. A frustum plane test is conservative: sphere reject if s < − r ; AABB reject if s < − ∑ i | n i | e i .
  • Perspective-correct attributes interpolate as ∑ i λ i ( a i / w i ) / ∑ i λ i / w i ; depth has its own projected interpolation rule.
  • Projection derivation · Depth precision · Raster mapping · Frustum culling · Interpolation

Time and calibrated image formation

  • Track jittered and unjittered projection state. Intrinsics map optical-camera rays to pixels; extrinsics map between world and optical camera. Lens distortion is a separate nonlinear map with a valid domain and inversion method.
  • Thin-lens rays originate across the aperture and converge on the focus plane. CoC and depth-of-field limits depend on an explicit sensor blur criterion. Aperture and shutter affect collected light; ISO-like gain does not add photons.
  • Stereo depth follows Z = f x B / Δ x for a rectified pinhole pair. Equal image areas on a sphere do not imply equal solid angles.

Figures and their full explanations

  • C11-1 · Depth mapping and floating-point spacing work together — RH camera looks down −Z, zero-to-one depth, n=0.1 and finite conventional far=100000; reversed curve uses infinite far. Right: error after rounding depth to binary32 and moving to its next representable value, including rounding and inversion effects. This is not a complete GPU transform-error model.

  • C11-2 · Culling is a conservative half-space test — This s–x cross-section has near=1, far=5 and ±45° side planes. For an inward unit plane normal, a sphere is outside only when signed centre distance < −radius. For an AABB use r = |nx|ex + |ny|ey + |nz|ez. Passing each plane does not prove exact intersection with the whole frustum.

  • C11-3 · Attributes follow reciprocal clip w — Vertex clip w values are (1,4,4); attribute values are (0,1,1). At the screen centroid the affine value is 2/3 and the corrected value is 1/3. Use sum(λ a/w) / sum(λ/w); depth-buffer interpolation has its own projected-depth rule.

  • C11-4 · Stereo turns pixel uncertainty into depth uncertainty — Rectified pinhole pair: focal length 800 pixels, baseline 0.064 m, disparity noise σd=0.25 pixel. Z=fB/d and σZ≈Z²σd/(fB). This local propagation assumes corresponding points, small noise and valid calibration; it is not a perception model.

  • C11-5 · Focus distance, focal length and aperture are different controls — Paraxial 50 mm lens at f/2 focused at 2 m: 1/F=1/u+1/v and c=D|v0/v−1|, with D=F/N. The 0.03 mm threshold is an explicitly chosen viewing criterion. This excludes diffraction, aberrations and sensor processing.

  • C11-6 · Distortion is a map with a domain, not just a coefficient list — Normalized Brown-style radial example xd=x(1+k₁r²), yd=y(1+k₁r²). This displayed domain has positive radial derivative 1+3k₁r², so the local radial map remains monotone. Larger radii can fold; numerical undistortion requires domain and convergence checks.

  • C11-7 · Image coordinates need a sampling-measure conversion — For longitude λ and latitude φ, dω=cosφ dλ dφ. Equal-width equirectangular image rows shrink toward the poles. Ray-differential Jacobians generalize the local image-area-to-solid-angle conversion; they do not by themselves specify lens-area or shutter-time probability.

  • C11-8 · Residual count does not guarantee observable motion — Toy Lucas–Kanade information matrices H=sum(∇I∇Iᵀ): repeated horizontal gradients give eigenvalues (0,3); diverse gradients give (1,3). Small eigenvalues expose weak directions. Camera-calibration gauge freedoms require a larger Jacobian model and explicit constraints.
