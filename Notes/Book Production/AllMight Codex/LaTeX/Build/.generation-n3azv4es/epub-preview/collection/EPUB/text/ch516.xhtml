Stable probability normalization and log-sum-exp

Curriculum depth: Developed · First seen: Phase 10 · Latest development: Phase 10

Definition and mechanism

To normalize nonnegative weights, divide each by their sum; direct summation can overflow even when the normalized probabilities are representable. Scale by the largest weight before summing. For log weights a i , compute m + log ⁡ ∑ i e a i − m , where m = max i a i . Subtracting the maximum prevents exponential overflow, and the result can be used to form softmax probabilities. This assumes at least one finite, valid weight and requires an explicit policy for all-zero, negative, NaN, or infinite inputs. Underflow of very small normalized probabilities is a separate concern from overflow; do not silently equate zero after underflow with mathematically impossible. For log weights ( 1000 , 1001 , 1000 ) , subtracting 1001 keeps exponentials bounded while leaving the normalized probabilities unchanged.
