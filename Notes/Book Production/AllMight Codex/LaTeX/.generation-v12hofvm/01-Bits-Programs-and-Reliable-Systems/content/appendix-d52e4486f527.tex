\section{Ownership and non-owning views}\label{appendix-d52e4486f527-ownership-and-non-owning-views}

\textbf{Curriculum depth:} Developed · \textbf{First seen:} C003 · \textbf{Latest development:} C010

\subsection{Definition and mechanism}\label{appendix-d52e4486f527-definition-and-mechanism}

By-value copies use member semantics, not recursive deep cloning. Rule-Zero composition delegates ownership to members; raw owners must coordinate transfer and release. Copying a pointer/view/handle is not acquiring ownership.

Copying span/string\_view or capturing a view by value copies only a descriptor. An owning string capture supplies independent storage; a view or handle does not automatically pin a deferred resource.

The inline C++ laboratory distinguishes NamedVector::to\_array, which copies values into a new array, from ArrayVector::view, which aliases actual array elements without owning them. Neither span nor pointer extends backing lifetime. Ownership and RAII are introduced as next-depth connections, not yet a full exception-safety treatment.

M010 makes the distinction operational: an owner is responsible for release; a borrow only accesses. \texttt{std::unique\_ptr} expresses exclusive ownership, \texttt{std::shared\_ptr} retains a shared lifetime, and \texttt{std::weak\_ptr} observes without extending that lifetime. Smart pointers do not remove the borrow/invalidation rules of raw pointers obtained from them. See \hyperref[appendix-69739d3ab49b]{RAII and resource handles} and \hyperref[appendix-51602cabe815]{Exclusive and shared smart-pointer ownership}.

An owner controls a resource's validity and release; a view merely refers to it. Vector owns its element buffer; span does not copy or prolong that buffer's life. Owner destruction or invalidating reallocation can dangle the span. A reader retaining a span needs stable live backing for every read, not only at construction.
