\section*{Matrix norms and numerical error}
\section{Matrix norms and numerical error}\label{matrix-norms-and-numerical-error}

The spectral norm is the largest 2-norm stretch and equals the largest singular value. The Frobenius norm measures aggregate squared entry energy. Residuals, orthogonality errors, and condition numbers need a declared norm and scale; a small residual alone need not imply a sensitive solution is accurate.

\textbf{Use it when:} setting scaled tolerances or interpreting numerical diagnostics.

\textbf{Developed in Phase 8:} The spectral norm measures worst-case amplification, while the Frobenius norm accumulates squared energy across entries. \subsection{A matrix norm can protect a geometry bound}\label{a-matrix-norm-can-protect-a-geometry-bound}

A local sphere under a linear transform is generally an ellipsoid. The Frobenius norm times its original radius gives a safe, possibly loose enclosing radius. Taking only the longest matrix column can fail under shear. \href{Continuous\%20Notes/University\%20-\%20Worlds,\%20Images\%20and\%20Inference\%20-\%20Graphics,\%20Vision\%20and\%20Photography\#A\%20line\%20corridor\%20can\%20be\%20a\%20cheap\%20prefilter\%20for\%20shadow-caster\%20work}{The culling example} derives the bound, demonstrates the shear counterexample, and links its geometric figure. This connects numerical matrix norms to conservative rejection decisions.
