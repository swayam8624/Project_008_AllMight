Axial depth and Euclidean range

Curriculum depth: Developed · First seen: Phase 11 · Latest development: Phase 11

Definition and mechanism

Axial depth measures distance along the camera’s forward axis; Euclidean range measures distance from the camera centre along the viewing ray. With a right-handed view frame looking along − Z , positive axial depth is d = − z , while range is r = x 2 + y 2 + z 2 . They agree only on the optical axis. For a unit ray direction q with q z < 0 , a range measurement converts to axial depth as d = − r q z . A range sensor and a perspective depth buffer may therefore encode different quantities. Applying a depth-buffer formula directly to a range image distorts reconstructed geometry away from the image centre. At view point ( 3 , 4 , − 12 ) , axial depth is 12 while range is 13 , a visible distinction off-axis.
