Convergence order and Richardson extrapolation

Curriculum depth: Developed · First seen: Phase 10 · Latest development: Phase 10

Definition and mechanism

If an approximation has leading error Q h = Q ∗ + C h p + O ( h p + 1 ) , halving the step reduces that term by about 2 p . Comparing results at h , h / 2 , and h / 4 estimates the observed order; Richardson extrapolation combines two resolutions to cancel the leading term when the assumed order is valid. The method is diagnostic, not magic: measurements must be in the asymptotic regime, the same mathematical quantity must be computed at each resolution, and roundoff or stochastic noise must not dominate. For Euler integration, first-order refinement predicts the leading error to halve. Record the sequence and residuals rather than reporting an order from one pair alone. Ratios near an expected power provide evidence, but one noisy ratio is not a convergence study.
