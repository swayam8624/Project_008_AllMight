Optical flow and time to contact

Curriculum depth: Developed · First seen: University courses · Latest development: University courses

Definition and mechanism

Optical flow is the apparent 2D motion of image features between frames. Under brightness constancy, a tracked point keeps approximately the same intensity as it moves, yielding the constraint I x u + I y v + I t = 0 , where spatial derivatives I x , I y and temporal derivative I t are measured from the image and ( u , v ) is image velocity. One equation cannot determine two unknown components at a single pixel, so algorithms add neighborhood smoothness, feature tracking, or multiple scales. Flow can reveal camera motion, object motion, and looming. For an object approaching at speed V , its image size grows at a rate related to depth; the ratio of size to its time derivative estimates time to contact without separately knowing absolute scale under simplifying assumptions. Texture, occlusion, and illumination changes violate brightness constancy.
