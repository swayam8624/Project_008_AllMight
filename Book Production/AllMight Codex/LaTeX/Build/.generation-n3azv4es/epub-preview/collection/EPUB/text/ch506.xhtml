PCA and covariance eigensystems

Center sample rows, then find the orthogonal directions of greatest variance. For centered X , SVD gives directions in V , scores in U Σ , and covariance eigenvalues σ i 2 / ( N − 1 ) . Units and feature scaling change the question; near-equal eigenvalues make individual axes unstable.

Use it when: describing point clouds, reducing dimensions, or estimating a best-fit plane.

Developed in Phase 8: PCA first centers observations so the covariance measures variation around the sample mean. The right singular vectors of centered data give principal directions, U Σ gives scores, and squared singular values scaled by sample count give covariance eigenvalues; feature units and small eigengaps affect interpretation. centering and principal axes (Mathematics for Visual Computing, Part 1, Ch. 8) · plane fits and eigengaps (Mathematics for Visual Computing, Part 1, Ch. 8)

Phase 9–10 development: For a random vector X with finite second moments, covariance is Σ = E [ ( X − μ ) ( X − μ ) T ] . For every vector v , v T Σ v = E [ ( v T ( X − μ ) ) 2 ] ≥ 0 , so covariance is positive semidefinite. Its eigenvectors give uncorrelated principal directions and the eigenvalues give variance along them. Whitening rescales nonzero-variance directions and needs a stated policy for zero or tiny eigenvalues. A Gaussian kernel used to model rendering footprints is a spatial distribution model, not automatically a statement about epistemic uncertainty.
