Matrix norms and numerical error

The spectral norm is the largest 2-norm stretch and equals the largest singular value. The Frobenius norm measures aggregate squared entry energy. Residuals, orthogonality errors, and condition numbers need a declared norm and scale; a small residual alone need not imply a sensitive solution is accurate.

Use it when: setting scaled tolerances or interpreting numerical diagnostics.

Developed in Phase 8: The spectral norm measures worst-case amplification, while the Frobenius norm accumulates squared energy across entries. A matrix norm can protect a geometry bound

A local sphere under a linear transform is generally an ellipsoid. The Frobenius norm times its original radius gives a safe, possibly loose enclosing radius. Taking only the longest matrix column can fail under shear. The culling example derives the bound, demonstrates the shear counterexample, and links its geometric figure. This connects numerical matrix norms to conservative rejection decisions.
