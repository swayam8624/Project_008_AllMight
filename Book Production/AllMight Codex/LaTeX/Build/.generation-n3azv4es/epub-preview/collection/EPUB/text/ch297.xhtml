PDE fields and discrete Laplacians

Curriculum depth: Developed · First seen: University courses · Latest development: University courses

Definition and mechanism

A partial differential equation constrains a field through spatial and temporal derivatives. Laplace’s equation Δ u = 0 seeks a harmonic field whose value matches its neighborhood average; Poisson’s equation adds a source term. On a regular grid, finite differences produce the five-point stencil with four neighbor weights and a negative center weight. On a triangle mesh, cotangent weights encode angles and a mass matrix normalizes by vertex area. Boundary conditions supply essential data: Dirichlet fixes values, Neumann fixes normal flux, and Robin combines them. Neumann Laplace systems require zero net flux and a pinned value or mean because constants lie in the nullspace. Explicit heat updates have a stability-limited time step; implicit updates solve a sparse system. PDE algorithms separate field representation, spatial operator, boundary conditions, and time integration.
