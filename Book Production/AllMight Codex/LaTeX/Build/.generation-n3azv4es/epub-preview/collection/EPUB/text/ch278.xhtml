Monte Carlo rendering estimators and variance

Curriculum depth: Developed · First seen: University courses · Latest development: Phase 10

Definition and mechanism

Monte Carlo integration estimates an integral by averaging random samples with importance weights. For target integral I = ∫ f ( x ) d x and samples drawn from density p ( x ) , the estimator is I ^ = N − 1 ∑ k = 1 N f ( x k ) / p ( x k ) . It is unbiased when samples cover every region where f is nonzero and the density is correct. Its variance falls approximately as 1 / N , so halving standard error takes about four times as many samples. Importance sampling chooses p to follow the magnitude of the integrand; multiple importance sampling combines strategies such as light sampling and BSDF sampling with weights that avoid overtrusting a strategy that rarely finds a contribution. Path tracing applies these estimators to light paths, while rare caustics and narrow visibility openings remain high-variance cases.

Phase 9–10 development

For independent samples from density p , the sample average estimates the integral using terms f ( X i ) / p ( X i ) where p is positive on the integrand support. Unbiasedness requires the support condition and integrability; finite variance additionally requires a finite second moment of f / p . A variance estimate describes sampling spread, not systematic bias. Dependent samples add covariance terms, and multiple-importance weights combine proposal densities while preserving mixture support. A confidence interval is justified only when its sampling and moment assumptions are stated.
