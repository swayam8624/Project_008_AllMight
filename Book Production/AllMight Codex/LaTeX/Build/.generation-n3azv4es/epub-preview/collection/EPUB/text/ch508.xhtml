Stable quadratic root evaluation

Curriculum depth: Developed · First seen: Phase 10 · Latest development: Phase 10

Definition and mechanism

The direct quadratic formula can lose nearly all significant digits in one root when it subtracts nearly equal values. For real coefficients with nonzero leading coefficient and a valid nonnegative discriminant, compute a signed large-magnitude numerator first: q = − 1 2 ( b + copysign ( b 2 − 4 a c , b ) ) , then obtain roots as q / a and c / q . The second root follows from the product relation x 1 x 2 = c / a . This avoids cancellation in the small root, but it does not prevent overflow in the discriminant or cover degenerate coefficients, nonfinite inputs, or q = 0 ; a robust solver also scales inputs and handles those cases explicitly. For example, the small root of x 2 + 10 8 x + 1 = 0 is near − 10 − 8 , exactly the root most endangered by direct subtraction.
