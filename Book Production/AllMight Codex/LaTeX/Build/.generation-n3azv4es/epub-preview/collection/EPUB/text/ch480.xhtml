Symmetric positive-definite matrices and Cholesky

A real symmetric matrix A is positive definite if every nonzero x has positive quadratic energy x T A x . Then A = L L T with nonsingular triangular L . Positive semidefinite permits zero energy directions and may be singular, so Cholesky’s assumptions and failure status must be respected.

Use it when: covariance, Gram systems, energy minimization, or an SPD solve is established.

Developed in Phase 8: Positive definiteness means every nonzero direction has positive quadratic energy, which permits a Cholesky factor and stable triangular solves. The continuation derives the positive pivots, then broadens to L D L T when a symmetric matrix is indefinite and explains how a failed pivot reflects the assumptions or numerical scale.
