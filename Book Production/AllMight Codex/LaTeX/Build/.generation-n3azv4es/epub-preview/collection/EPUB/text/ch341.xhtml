Light spectra and radiometry

Curriculum depth: Developed · First seen: XR01 · Latest development: University courses

Definition and mechanism

Light models distinguish wave propagation, photon energy and geometrical rays. Radiometry describes physical flux/radiance/irradiance; photometry adds standardized visual weighting. RGB requires an interpretation and cannot identify a unique spectrum.

University course development

Radiometry distinguishes total radiant flux Φ in watts, irradiance E in watts per square metre, radiant intensity I in watts per steradian, and radiance L in watts per square metre per steradian measured with projected source area. A spectrum distributes the energy over wavelength; a three-channel RGB value is a measurement in a chosen color system and does not uniquely identify that spectrum. Solid angle measures directional extent rather than physical area. Consequently an area-light sample requires the projected-area and inverse-square Jacobian when expressed as a directional sample. Free-space radiance remains constant along an unobstructed ray, while irradiance from a point-like source changes with geometry. Photometry instead applies standardized visual weighting: luminous flux uses lumens, illuminance lux, and luminous intensity candela. These distinctions prevent treating brightness, exposure, flux and radiance as interchangeable scalar labels.
