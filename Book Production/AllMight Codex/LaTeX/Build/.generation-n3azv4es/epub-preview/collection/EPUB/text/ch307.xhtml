Homogeneous clipping and perspective interpolation

Curriculum depth: Developed · First seen: University courses · Latest development: University courses

Definition and mechanism

After a vertex is transformed, its clip-space coordinate is ( x c , y c , z c , w c ) . Clip primitives against the view volume before perspective division; under the conventional OpenGL clip convention the inequalities are − w c ≤ x c , y c , z c ≤ w c . A crossing edge is split by interpolating its homogeneous endpoints, which preserves the projective line and avoids dividing by a small or negative w too early. Only surviving points are divided by w c to enter normalized device coordinates. Attributes then need perspective-correct interpolation: screen-space barycentric weights interpolate a / w and 1 / w , and the fragment value is their ratio. For a triangle with weights λ i , a = ( ∑ i λ i a i / w i ) / ( ∑ i λ i / w i ) . Direct linear interpolation in screen space is correct only for affine quantities or equal depth; otherwise textures warp. Clip-space depth conventions and the near/far interval vary by API and projection setup, so the inequalities must match the active pipeline.

Phase 11 camera development

Clipping and perspective-correct interpolation preserve projective geometry, while texture filtering asks how much surface area maps to a pixel. Neighboring camera rays approximate the image-to-surface footprint. At grazing angles one pixel can cover a long, thin region; a center-only texel lookup may alias detail even when attributes interpolate correctly. Use footprint estimates to select mip levels or anisotropic filters, while respecting visibility edges where derivatives jump. Ray differentials aid sampling but do not replace clipping or multisample coverage. Source basis: PBRT differential-ray texture filtering. Executable raster-state continuation

The project now carries two complementary CPU witnesses. tools/labs/raster_reference_lab.cpp owns edge coverage, barycentrics, perspective-correct interpolation and depth ordering. tools/labs/raster_pipeline_state_lab.cpp continues the same pipeline through a homogeneous clip-plane intersection, an explicit four-sample coverage mask, stencil compare/pass update and premultiplied source-over blending.

That separation is deliberate: clipping happens in homogeneous coordinates before divide-by- w ; sample ownership happens after screen mapping; stencil/depth are stateful per-fragment/sample decisions; blending combines a surviving source with an existing destination. Treating all of them as “rasterization” without preserving these boundaries makes debugging nearly impossible.
