\section*{Research reconstruction: nonlinear least squares with outliers}
\noindent\textbf{Source lineage:} Gauss--Newton and
Levenberg--Marquardt methods; the
\href{https://ceres-solver.readthedocs.io/latest/nnls_modeling.html}
{Ceres Solver nonlinear-least-squares modeling documentation}
and \href{https://ceres-solver.readthedocs.io/latest/tutorial.html}
{robust curve-fitting tutorial}. Our small IRLS/Levenberg-like
solver is independently authored; it is not Ceres code.

\paragraph{Define the measurement problem.}
Suppose samples \((x_i,y_i)\) follow
\(y_i=a\exp(bx_i)+c+\epsilon_i\). For parameters
\(\theta=(a,b,c)^T\), write residuals
\[
r_i(\theta)=a e^{bx_i}+c-y_i.
\]
The Jacobian row is
\[
J_i=\begin{pmatrix}
e^{bx_i}&a x_i e^{bx_i}&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This derivation is a contract: numerical finite differences
or automatic differentiation of the same residual should
agree within justified tolerance away from overflow.

\paragraph{Derive the update.}
Linearizing around \(\theta\) gives
\(r(\theta+\Delta)\approx r+J\Delta\).
Ordinary least squares minimizes \(\frac12\lVert r\rVert^2\);
its normal-system approximation is
\(J^TJ\,\Delta=-J^Tr\).
Direct normal equations may magnify conditioning; production
solvers use better factorizations and regularization strategies.
To keep a small educational step well defined, use a positive
damping term:
\[
(J^TWJ+\lambda I)\Delta=-J^TWr.
\]
For the Huber loss with threshold \(\delta>0\), a common
IRLS approximation assigns
\[
w_i=\begin{cases}
1,&|r_i|\le\delta,\\
\delta/|r_i|,&|r_i|>\delta.
\end{cases}
\]
The corresponding scalar cost is
\(\rho_\delta(r)=\frac12r^2\) in the quadratic region and
\(\rho_\delta(r)=\delta(|r|-\delta/2)\) outside it.
Always measure trial improvement using the \emph{actual robust
objective}, not the weighted quadratic approximation that
generated the step.

\paragraph{A controlled outlier experiment.}
Our companion creates 35 synthetic observations from
\((a,b,c)=(2,-0.7,0.5)\) with a small deterministic sinusoidal
perturbation and one declared gross outlier. We fit the same
observations with three methods: plain nonlinear least squares
from SciPy, Huber-robust SciPy least squares, and our own
damped IRLS method. Each estimates \(\theta\), but the
underlying estimand depends on the objective and noise model.

\paragraph{Verification and limits.}
Use numerical Jacobian comparisons, final parameter differences,
robust-objective values, gradient norms and stopping reasons.
Seed initial conditions explicitly; report whether a solver hit
its iteration limit or a stationary point. A robust loss does
\emph{not} guarantee recovery under arbitrary contamination,
bad initialization, non-identifiable parameters or an incorrect
forward model. A successful 35-point synthetic experiment
is not a production claim about bundle adjustment.

\begin{booktwolab}[Least squares]
\paragraph{C++23 checkpoint: what ordinary least squares can and cannot do.}
The command \texttt{book02\_workbench fit} uses the C++23 sample type,
a centered mean, and the two sums
\[
 S_{xx}=\sum_i(x_i-\bar{x})^2,\qquad
 S_{xy}=\sum_i(x_i-\bar{x})(y_i-\bar{y})
\]
to recover \(a=S_{xy}/S_{xx}\) and \(b=\bar{y}-a\bar{x}\) for
\(y=ax+b\). It returns an error state if \(S_{xx}=0\), because a
vertical set of samples cannot identify a finite slope in this model.
The first test fits the exactly constructed line \(y=2x+1\);
the independent expected answer is therefore \(a=2,b=1\).

\end{booktwolab}

\begin{booktwopitfall}[An outlier is not a roundoff error]
Now insert an extreme outlier. Explain how one high-leverage sample
can dominate squared residuals despite centered accumulation. Better
summation reduces cancellation; it does not make least squares robust
to contaminated data. The separate RANSAC and robust-loss projects
earlier in this book are the correct comparison, with thresholds and
sampling assumptions explicitly disclosed. A further challenge is to
replace ordinary centered regression with Householder QR and compare
both methods on a badly conditioned overdetermined system.

\end{booktwopitfall}
