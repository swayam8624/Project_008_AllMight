\section*{Visual laboratory: a matrix changes a shape}
\noindent A linear transformation does more than rearrange symbols. Here each point
\(\mathbf u(\theta)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)^T\) on the unit circle becomes
\(\mathbf p(\theta)=A\mathbf u(\theta)\) under
\(A=\begin{pmatrix}2&0.6\\0&0.45\end{pmatrix}\).
The longer axis in the image is a consequence of anisotropic scaling and shear;
it is not a measured dataset.

\noindent\textbf{Reconstruct it:} compute \(A^TA\), its eigenvectors,
and the square roots of its eigenvalues. Overlay the two principal axes;
then check that applying \(A^{-1}\) recovers the original points within a
reported floating-point tolerance.
