\section*{Symmetric positive-definite matrices and Cholesky}
\chapter{Symmetric positive-definite matrices and Cholesky}\label{appendix-50d2ea3c9b26-symmetric-positive-definite-matrices-and-cholesky}

A real symmetric matrix \(A\) is positive definite if every nonzero \(x\) has positive quadratic energy \(x^\mathsf T A x\). Then \(A=LL^\mathsf T\) with nonsingular triangular \(L\). Positive semidefinite permits zero energy directions and may be singular, so Cholesky's assumptions and failure status must be respected.

\textbf{Use it when:} covariance, Gram systems, energy minimization, or an SPD solve is established.

\textbf{Developed in Phase 8:} Positive definiteness means every nonzero direction has positive quadratic energy, which permits a Cholesky factor and stable triangular solves. The continuation derives the positive pivots, then broadens to \(LDL^\mathsf T\) when a symmetric matrix is indefinite and explains how a failed pivot reflects the assumptions or numerical scale. \href{Continuous\%20Notes/Computing\%20Foundations\%20-\%20From\%20Bits\%20to\%20C++\%20and\%20Systems\%20APIs\#Positive\%20definiteness\%20is\%20a\%20structural\%20promise}{Cholesky and positive energy} · \href{Continuous\%20Notes/Computing\%20Foundations\%20-\%20From\%20Bits\%20to\%20C++\%20and\%20Systems\%20APIs\#LDLT\%20reaches\%20symmetric\%20indefinite\%20matrices}{the symmetric-indefinite extension}
