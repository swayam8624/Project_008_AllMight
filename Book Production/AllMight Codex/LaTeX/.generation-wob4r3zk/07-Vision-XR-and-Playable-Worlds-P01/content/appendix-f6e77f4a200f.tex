\section{Gaussian elimination and pivoting}\label{appendix-f6e77f4a200f-gaussian-elimination-and-pivoting}

\hypertarget{appendix-f6e77f4a200f-gaussian-elimination-and-pivoting}{}

Equivalent elementary row operations expose pivots and triangular structure while preserving the solution set when applied to the full augmented system. Pivoting avoids zero or dangerously small divisors; it improves the calculation but does not cure an ill-conditioned problem.

\textbf{Use it when:} understanding REF/RREF, LU, consistency, rank, and triangular solves.

\textbf{Developed in Phase 8:} Elimination turns those geometric questions into auditable row operations on the augmented matrix. Pivots expose rank and free variables; partial pivoting chooses a safer divisor and tracks row exchanges, while residual and growth checks explain why pivoting cannot repair ill-conditioning. \href{../02-Mathematics-for-Visual-Computing-P01/main.pdf\#01-foundations-i-ch019-elimination-turns-the-geometry-into-an-auditable-calculation}{elimination steps (Mathematics for Visual Computing, Part 1, Ch. 3)} · \href{../02-Mathematics-for-Visual-Computing-P01/main.pdf\#01-foundations-i-ch019-pivoting-separates-algorithm-failure-from-matrix-failure}{pivoting and growth (Mathematics for Visual Computing, Part 1, Ch. 3)}
